## Dry structure and component density

The recovered global constructors set edge density to **1000** and plate density to **400**. These are native density coefficients; this checkpoint does not claim a complete SI mass/inertia model.

The previously reviewed `server_scene.vehicle.build_physics_shape` (52757266) adds edge capsules, eligible plate convex hulls and component-zone boxes to a compound shape. Component boxes use the definition's `m_density` field, reflected at offset 584. Overall visible bounding-box volume is therefore insufficient to calculate complete vehicle mass: zone geometry, eligibility, separate bodies and native shape construction matter.

## Liquid tanks carry separate ballast bodies

Tank liquid mass is calculated by `get_fluid_mass` (58733303), summing each species quantity times `_get_fluid_density` (58717295):

```
liquid_mass = water + 0.8 * (petrol + oil + pollutant)
```

The coefficients are **1.0** for Water enum 0 and **0.8** for Petrol/Oil/Pollutant enums 1/2/3. Quantities use the game's litre scale. Thus equal quantities of these fuels/oils and water do not contribute equal ballast mass. Unknown enum values do not gain an invented density fallback in this report.

`liquid_tank._rebuild_fluid_physics` (56382798) removes/destroys an existing fluid constraint and body, then creates a **one-grid-unit box** as a separate physics object. It positions that body through component/body transforms and connects it to the chassis with a distance constraint. The group mask is set separately. Full native collision-group interpretation and constraint solver behavior remain outside this trace.

Tank tick (56405131) periodically updates this body's mass to:

```
max(0.01, liquid_mass)
```

When its distance constraint is active, the allowed distance becomes:

```
minimum = 0
maximum = 0.08 * clamp(1 - fill_factor, 0.01, 1)
```

The permitted maximum therefore decreases from **0.08 m** toward **0.0008 m** as fill rises through the ordinary 0..1 range. It does not reach literal zero. This is a constrained ballast body, rather than simply adding the current litres to the dry chassis's density.

The update counter resets to **60** when zero, otherwise decrements. That yields an update every **61 tick invocations** after a reset, provided the path keeps running. This report does not assign an unverified wall-clock interval. Creation/scene-presence guards apply. Debug fill/empty assignments occur later in tick; they do not retroactively change the earlier mass calculation in the same invocation.

Filled buoyancy surfaces have another separate body/constraint path. `surface.update_physics` (62372884) sets its body mass from filled volume times the configured coefficient and updates its distance constraint from fill ratio. Reflection distinguishes `m_volume_total`, `m_volume_filled`, `_m_volume_physics` and `_m_volume_constraint`. `update_server` (62378384) reads the chassis transform, velocities, center of mass and mass, then conditionally calculates/applies forces and updates these surface bodies. Tank ballast and hull flooding should not be treated as one interchangeable mechanism; complete buoyancy coefficients and force closure remain open.

## Native mass and shape boundary

Selected registration bytes now bind:

| API | Target RVA |
| --- | --- |
| `physics.object.get_mass` | `0x14bf50` |
| `physics.object.get_center_of_mass` | `0x14c0b0` |
| `physics.object.set_mass` | `0x14c9a0` |
| `physics.object.get_transform` | `0x14be90` |
| Box creation overload with dimensions/offset/density | `0x14d950` |
| Capsule creation overload | `0x14da60` |
| Vector/array convex-hull creation overloads | `0x14de10` / `0x14de20` |

Both pointer-store registration branches were inspected. Some targets are leaf stubs or compiler-split routines, so manual selected spans remain explicitly labeled and are not claimed to be recovered complete function boundaries.

The mass getter returns zero without an underlying body; otherwise it obtains a native mass-property result, reads its first **f32** value and widens it to f64. The setter narrows the requested mass to f32, stores it and obtains the shape's base mass properties. When base mass is positive it scales twelve entries of the associated property block by **new mass / base shape mass**, then calls the native update. This is evidence of mass-property rescaling, not a complete tensor-layout or solver proof. The getter and setter are not universal f64-precision storage paths.

The center-of-mass getter copies three underlying body coordinates, or returns zero if that body is absent. The transform getter separately copies twelve cached f64 values. Complete cache synchronization, body-frame conventions and every solver update remain open; do not substitute the saved creation origin for center of mass.

The convex-hull vector and array stubs jump to distinct bounded converters (`0x10d2a0`, `0x10d540`). Both convert supplied vec3 f64 coordinates to native f32 point storage and narrow the density before delegating to a common shape creator (`0x10dc30`). The box converter halves the supplied dimensions before its native settings construction; the capsule converter halves the supplied length. Native hull generation, shape margins and all mass/inertia virtual implementations remain additional work. These conversions supplement the [separate visible/collision plate measurements](PLATE-SURFACES-20261021.md).

Vehicle `build` (52741190) passes the chassis physics object's mass, when it is in the scene, to the torque-node-container builder. This connects the [vehicle-mass-dependent torque model](POWERTRAIN-20261020.md) to native body mass. It is not evidence that every constrained ballast body's mass is automatically included in that single chassis getter.

## Normal crash damage uses impulse gates

Vehicle contact handler 53189119 reads the reflected **f32 `m_contact_impulse`**, not contact speed. The reviewed ordinary path first excludes a creature-handled case. It can emit a contact event when the maximum estimated impulse is **greater than 500**.

For each impulse, the damage path requires:

1. Impulse **at least 4000**.
2. The integer conversion of **impulse * 0.0008** is **at least 5**.
3. Damage radius comes from the integer conversion of **impulse * 0.0002**, clamped to **0..2** grid units.

The two conversions are actual x64 **CVTSD2SI** instructions. Their rounding depends on the configured floating-point control state; the checker does not assume a universal halfway rule. In particular, 4000 is the first gate, not a complete guarantee that damage occurs there. The clamped interpolation using 4000/10000 also appears in the method, but the reviewed calls do not receive that temporary as a damage-strength argument.

The contact position is transformed into body coordinates and native grid coordinates before both `damage_destructive` and `damage_functional` are called. Prevent-damage and positive spawn-protection guards are applied in those methods.

Destructive damage (52951564) queries a region around that grid position, removes ordinary hit components, and handles components marked **devolvable** through a separate remove/recreate subdivision path. It also removes surviving hit edges and plates, then requests physics and structural-island rebuilding. Functional damage (52986438) applies component damage and has separate glass-impact/break-state handling. Complete subdivision arithmetic, octree candidate precision and all component damage overrides are not certified by this checkpoint.

The reviewed contact-to-edge-removal path has global impulse/radius gates; it does not look up a separate per-edge health or strength multiplier. Wider frame geometry affects shape construction, but these methods do not establish a blanket “thicker beam survives a larger impulse” rule. This is a scoped finding about normal crash handling, not proof that no other strength or damage mechanism exists anywhere in the game.
